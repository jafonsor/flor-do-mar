# EVE Online: Line-of-Fire Occlusion, Missile Interception, Unintended Targets, Friendly Fire, and Cover

Research section for the game-design report. Every claim carries a citation. Unverified claims are marked **UNCERTAIN**.

**Source quality key:** *Primary* = eveonline.com news/patch content or archived CCP developer posts. *Partner wiki* = EVE University Wiki (CCP's official EVE Partner wiki, community-edited). *SDE mirror* = third-party mirrors of CCP's published static data export (attribute values and item descriptions authored by CCP). *Community* = player forum posts, labelled as such.

---

## A. Line-of-fire occlusion: **not modelled**

- **Turrets do not fire a travelling projectile.** A turret cycle resolves immediately as a single random decimal rolled against a computed hit chance; the same number also sets the damage multiplier. Hit chance is a function of **range (optimal/falloff), tracking (angular velocity), turret tracking, and target signature radius — and nothing else**. There is no occlusion, intercept, or "something in the way" term anywhere in the equation. [EVE Uni Wiki — Turret mechanics](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Turret_mechanics)
- The random number is used twice: first to decide hit/miss against the computed chance, second to convert the roll into a 0.5–1.5 damage multiplier (with a 1% chance of a 300% wrecking shot). The system is explicitly described as "not fully realistic, as it only considers the relative movement between the attacker and the target". [EVE Uni Wiki — Turret mechanics](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Turret_mechanics)
- Turrets are described as firing "a stream of stuff — pellets, pulses, ray beams — in a straight line", and they "reach their targets immediately", versus missiles which "fly out to their targets". The straight line is a client-side visual; the resolution is a roll on the locked target. [EVE Uni Wiki — Weapons](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Weapons)
- **Missiles are real objects, but they only ever hit their locked target.** A missile leaves the launcher, homes on the target, and explodes when it crosses the target's signature radius — "if it encounters the target during that time, it will explode – otherwise it will vanish." There is no stray detonation on bystanders. A missile's "explosion radius" and "explosion velocity" are damage-application *math attributes*, not a real-space blast that can catch other ships. [EVE Uni Wiki — Missile mechanics](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Missile_mechanics)
- **Conclusion: a shot aimed at ship A never damages ship B** via turrets or missiles. A third ship crossing the line of fire changes nothing — it is not in the calculation. If it is merely *closer* to the shooter, the only observable effect is that the client may draw the beam or projectile trail through it.
- **Community confirmation (label: unofficial).** Players have repeatedly requested obstructing objects and line-of-sight; the feature does not exist. The design argument raised in the thread is that EVE ships aim themselves on a server tick with no player control over aim, so occlusion would add an uncontrollable variable to fleet combat. Feature-request thread, 2022: [Objects obstructing line of sight?](https://forums.eveonline.com/t/objects-obstrcuting-line-of-sight/350354). Posts in that thread are player opinion, not CCP policy.
- **The nearest thing to line geometry in EVE is a Titan superweapon, not occlusion.** A Titan **Lance** "target[s] a 2.5km-radius, 200km long line" and deals damage to all ships within the beam; a **Reaper** sweeps a 5 km-radius beam between two targeted points in space; the **Bosonic Field Generator** covers a 30 km-long cone. These are explicit AoE templates anchored to a line or cone — they do not care what stands between the titan and the target, and all of them also emit a 10 km radius, 35,000 GJ energy-neutralising pulse (friendly or hostile) when they begin charging. [EVE Uni Wiki — Titans](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Titans)
- Doomsday (single-target) superweapons likewise resolve on the locked capital ship after a delay, with no intervening-object check. [EVE Uni Wiki — Titans](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Titans)

## B. Anti-missile interception: **bombs only, not missiles**

### Defender Missiles / Defender Launcher I — still in the game, repurposed in the December 2016 release

Primary source: archived CCP dev post by CCP Larrikin, [\[December\] Defender Missiles — Upcoming Feature and Change Feedback Center](https://forums-archive.eveonline.com/topic/501519).

- **The core change:** "Defender Missiles will **no longer shoot down missiles aimed at you**. Instead they will launch at a **random bomb (non-structure)** within its flight range. A single defender missile will kill any bomb." [CCP Larrikin, Dec 2016](https://forums-archive.eveonline.com/topic/501519)
- **Random, side-blind targeting.** Asked whether Defender Missiles distinguish friendly from non-friendly bombs, CCP Larrikin replied: "They do not, they target a random bomb within intercept range. It does not consider friendly or non-friendly bombs." Asked whether the selection is closest or random: "Its truly random." [CCP Larrikin, Dec 2016](https://forums-archive.eveonline.com/topic/501519)
- **Fitting and role limits:** the *Defender Launcher I* "may only be fit to Destroyer class vessels (Destroyers, Interdictors, Command Destroyers and Tactical Destroyers)"; **one per ship**; it does not require a launcher hardpoint; 10 CPU / 2 PG / 50 GJ per activation; **120-second reactivation timer**; Command Destroyers receive a 50% role bonus to reducing that reactivation timer. [CCP Larrikin, Dec 2016](https://forums-archive.eveonline.com/topic/501519)
- **Range and flight:** *Defender Missile I* has a base range of 30 km (45 km at maximum skills) and a flight time of 3 seconds. The *Defender Missiles* skill gives a 10% per level bonus to defender missile velocity, and "no other skills will effect Defender Missiles". On the December 2016 patch downtime, all existing defender missiles and their blueprints were converted to the new *Defender Missile I*. [CCP Larrikin, Dec 2016](https://forums-archive.eveonline.com/topic/501519)
- **No lock required; it fires itself.** "Once activated, it will scan local space for any bombs, and if it finds one within range, launch a defender missile to intercept it. If it doesn't find any bombs within range, it will still cycle." The current partner-wiki summary matches. [CCP Larrikin, Dec 2016](https://forums-archive.eveonline.com/topic/501519), [EVE Uni Wiki — Missiles](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Missiles)
- **Do not rely on them against structures.** "Defender missiles will never target standup bombs and standup guided bombs (bombs deployed by Upwell structures)." [EVE Uni Wiki — Bombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Bombs)
- **Community read (label: community).** Player discussion in 2024 treats the module as a specialized anti-stealth-bomber tool and notes that "one defender can be fit per destroyer" and that fleets should stagger activations so five defenders are not burned on the first bomb wave. [Official forum thread, Oct 2024](https://forums.eveonline.com/t/question-about-defender-missiles/463153)

### Smartbombs are the real missile defence

- **AoE radius and what it hits.** Smartbombs are "point-blank area-of-effect weapons" that "do damage to *everything* in a short distance (**~10km**) around the activating ship, **friend or foe**". In practice micro smartbombs have a 2 km range and small/medium/large run 3 km / 4 km / 5 km at T1 (faction large 6–7.5 km), with the class ceiling around 10 km. Damage ignores signature radius and speed and is mitigated only by resistances and by not being in the blast. [EVE Uni Wiki — Smartbombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Smartbombs)
- **Your own assets are not exempt:** "Your own drones, if they are within range, will also be damaged by a smartbomb." The activating ship itself is the sole exception ("They damage all ships within the explosion radius apart from the ship which used the smartbomb"), and smartbombs do not damage wrecks or Upwell structures, nor generate a structure-damage timer. [EVE Uni Wiki — Smartbombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Smartbombs)
- **Yes, they destroy your own in-flight missiles.** Missiles are real objects with structure HP: "While these entities are not visible on the overview nor selectable, they **can** decloak ships, and have structure hp that can be damaged or even be destroyed by area-of-effect damages like Smartbombs, Bombs, and Guided Bombs." Pilots are warned to "avoid destroying their missiles with their smartbombs by avoiding fitting or activating them at the same time". [EVE Uni Wiki — Missiles](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Missiles)
- **Firewalling.** "This feature also enabled a strategy known as 'Firewalling', in which multiple smartbombs are used to block out and destroy incoming missiles… it can greatly reduce a fleet's incoming (and outgoing) missile damage." [EVE Uni Wiki — Missiles](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Missiles), [EVE Uni Wiki — Smartbombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Smartbombs)
- **Highsec / safety-setting behaviour.** "In order to activate smartbombs in high security space, you need to set your safety settings to red." Highsec use "comes with a *very high* risk of hitting innocent passers-by, losing security status, and then being destroyed by CONCORD". In nullsec and wormhole space smartbombs work on full (green) safety. In lowsec, pilots run red safeties specifically so they can use smartbombs (using them does not itself make you a criminal — only unprovoked attacks on capsules do). [EVE Uni Wiki — Smartbombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Smartbombs), [EVE Uni Wiki — Safety settings](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Safety_settings)
- **Deployment restrictions.** Smartbombs "cannot be used close to stations, stargates, or acceleration gates" — the module returns a minimum-range error; the restriction is typically 5–6 km. [EVE Uni Wiki — Smartbombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Smartbombs)
- **AoE bypasses post-warp invulnerability.** A ship exiting warp is still "vulnerable to weapons such as Smartbombs during the final seconds of your warp deceleration", even though the Warp Invulnerability Timer otherwise protects it. [EVE Uni Wiki — Timers](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Timers)
- **Smartbombs also clear tackle infrastructure:** interdiction warp-disruption probes "can be destroyed by smartbombs". [EVE Uni Wiki — Tackling](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Tackling)

### Other point-defence-like mechanics

- **Bombs can be destroyed by other bombs.** "Like missiles, bombs have very few hit points, and can be destroyed by defender missiles, smartbombs, and even other bombs." Each of the four damage bomb types has a 99.8% resistance to its own damage type, which is why coordinated bomber wings standardize on one damage type — to avoid destroying friendly bombs. [EVE Uni Wiki — Bombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Bombs)
- **Standup Point Defense Battery.** A structure **Area Denial Module** with an item description reading: "The Standup Point Defense Battery is a system designed to provide close point defense capability to space structures, providing area denial as required by structure operators. The Point Defense Battery is designed as an integrated PDC network and can be installed as a module in Upwell technology structures." SDE attributes: fits Fortizar/Keepstar/Azbel/Sotiyo/Tatara (one module of this group, one online), 12 s activation, 1,500 GJ, 1,000 tf, 150,000 MW, 600 m³ capacity, 3-minute reload, 200 charges per cycle, **2,500 m area-of-effect radius**, **"Banned in High Sec Space: true"**, and it "does not function in high-security space". Ammunition is **Standup Flak Round I** — caseless rounds with an explosive strontium clathrates core and a megacyte primer, "designed to be fired en mass from structure defensive installations", dealing 250 each of EM/Explosive/Kinetic/Thermal. [EVE Ref — Standup Point Defense Battery I](https://everef.net/types/35926) (SDE mirror), [EVE Ref — Standup Flak Round I](https://everef.net/types/63195) (SDE mirror)
  - **UNCERTAIN:** whether the Standup Point Defense Battery explicitly intercepts and destroys incoming **missiles or bombs**, as opposed to simply dealing 2,500 m-radius AoE damage to ships/drones near the structure. The SDE exposes an area-of-effect radius and no missile-interception attribute; the item description says "point defense" and "area denial". I could not find a CCP statement or partner-wiki page confirming anti-missile behaviour either way. Treat as unconfirmed.
  - A related structure combat rig family exists — **Standup XL-Set Integrated Fighter and PD Network I / II** — indicating a fighter-plus-point-defence design intent at the structure level. [EVE Ref — Standup XL-Set Integrated Fighter and PD Network I](https://everef.net/types/37270) (SDE mirror). **UNCERTAIN:** its exact effect on missile or bomb interception.
- **Net structural finding:** EVE has an anti-**bomb** ship module (Defender Launcher, destroyers only, bombs only), anti-**bomb** structure rigs, and an anti-**everything-nearby** module (smartbomb). It has **no dedicated anti-missile system for player-launched missiles** other than smartbomb firewalling — a fact that follows directly from Defender Missiles having been reassigned to bombs in 2016. [CCP Larrikin, Dec 2016](https://forums-archive.eveonline.com/topic/501519), [EVE Uni Wiki — Missiles](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Missiles)

## C. Weapons that *do* hit unintended targets

### Smartbombs

- **Mechanic.** Untargeted, no lock, not a launched warhead — "the explosion they create originates from the ship, rather than a launched warhead". Hits all ships in radius except the activating ship; damage unaffected by the target's signature radius or speed. [EVE Uni Wiki — Smartbombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Smartbombs)
- **Friendly fire implications.** "Both friends and foes are damaged by smartbombs." In highsec and lowsec, care must be taken "not to unintentionally damage structures or ships protected by CONCORD, since this will affect the user's security status, and in high security will result in CONCORD intervention (i.e. the destruction of the offending ship)." Your own drones are hit too. [EVE Uni Wiki — Smartbombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Smartbombs)
- **Highsec CONCORD response.** Per Crimewatch, "activating any offensive module on another player's ship, capsule or assets that the player is not legitimately allowed to attack (war targets, for example), **including non-targeted modules like Smartbombs**, will provoke a Criminal Timer". In highsec a Criminal Timer means CONCORD spawns and attacks, gate/station sentries fire, and any player may fire on you without penalty. In lowsec, smartbombing a player's *ship* produces only a Suspect flag. [EVE Uni Wiki — Timers / Crimewatch](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Timers)
- **Fleet-relevant uses.** Pipe bombing (jump-bridging a fleet of smartbomb battleships onto an enemy fleet as it lands) deals "massive damage to the whole fleet at once with no lock on time" — a technique that is inherently indiscriminate. Rookie tacklers are advised to orbit large ships outside smartbomb range (5 km T1 / 6 km T2). [EVE Uni Wiki — Smartbombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Smartbombs), [EVE Uni Wiki — Turret mechanics](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Turret_mechanics)

### Bombs (stealth bombers)

- **Untargeted, orientation-aimed, 30 km straight line.** Bombs "do not require a locked target to be launched, and depend on the launching ship's orientation to aim". Once deployed they "travel forward from the front of the bomb launcher in a straight line, continuing until they reach a distance of 30km from the launch point, where they then detonate". Damage bombs travel at 2,500 m/s with a 12-second flight time; Void and Lockbreaker bombs at 4,000 m/s / 7.5 s; Focused Void at 2,000 m/s / 15 s. [EVE Uni Wiki — Bombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Bombs)
- **15 km sphere, indiscriminate.** "Upon detonation, bombs cause damage or other disrupting effects to **all ships within a 15km radius** of the detonation. These effects are **indiscriminate**, and are applied to all ships within the blast zone, **including any allies and even the launching ship**." Bombers are advised to break formation after launching and to track allied launch points. [EVE Uni Wiki — Bombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Bombs)
- **Damage model.** Base 5,800 damage of the bomb's type (raised from 5,200 on 2022-03-08), fixed 400 m explosion radius, scaled by the target's signature radius, mitigated by resistances; **unlike missiles, target speed does not matter**, so an MWD's signature bloom is what kills frigates. Lockbreaker Bombs apply ECM to all ships in the area, including friendlies, "so they should be used with care" and cleared with the FC first. [EVE Uni Wiki — Bombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Bombs), [CCP patch notes 20.03](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/patch-notes-version-20-03)
- **Banned in empire space.** "They are banned from use in empire space, and so can only be used in null sec or wormhole space." The SDE confirms the mechanism: **Bomb Launcher I** carries the attribute **"Banned in Empire Space: true"** together with `allowInFullyCorruptedLowSec: 1` (i.e. also permitted in lowsec systems at maximum corruption). One bomb launcher per stealth bomber; 2 m 40 s reactivation; a **minimum velocity of 1 m/s** is required before activation; 150 m³ capacity. [EVE Uni Wiki — Bombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Bombs), [EVE Ref — Bomb Launcher I](https://everef.net/types/27914) (SDE mirror)
- **Safety settings in corrupted lowsec.** "Low Security space with max corruption: These systems are special, as Bomb Launchers and Interdiction Sphere Launchers can be activated with green safety and can result in suspect or criminal timers." In nullsec and wormhole space bomb launchers work on full green safety. [EVE Uni Wiki — Safety settings](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Safety_settings)
- **Friendly-bomb fratricide is a real fleet problem.** "The interference of friendly bombs can be a very real concern during larger fleet operations", which is why wings standardize on one damage type (99.8% self-resistance). [EVE Uni Wiki — Bombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Bombs)

### Vorton Projectors / Arcing Vorton Projectors

- **Chain resolution.** Vorton Projectors "fire chain lightning which strikes the ship that is targeted and **arcs across up to nine additional random ships (or drones) within 10km of the first**". Despite the animation, the bounced entities are determined by range to the *primary* target, and the chain "**does not** bounce to chain further entities". "The Vorton Projectors will **never hit the ship that fires them**." [EVE Uni Wiki — Vorton Projectors](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Vorton_Projectors)
- **Damage model.** Uses missile damage mechanics (explosion radius/velocity) rather than tracking, so range and angular velocity between user and target do not affect application; deals EM and Kinetic damage; huge optimal range; fittable only to EDENCOM ships with a single turret slot. DRF values: small 0.40, medium 0.50, large 0.70. [EVE Uni Wiki — Vorton Projectors](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Vorton_Projectors), [EVE Uni Wiki — Missile mechanics](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Missile_mechanics)
- **Safety-setting interaction.** "In areas of space where crimewatch is in effect, the projectors will respect safety settings, and **with full safeties enabled will only bounce to legal targets**. As a consequence, however, bounces from this weapon can initiate Limited Engagements with suspects and outlaws, **even if they were not targeted**." [EVE Uni Wiki — Vorton Projectors](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Vorton_Projectors)
- **UNCERTAIN:** whether a Vorton chain can damage *fleetmates* in nullsec or wormhole space. Safety settings filter bounces to "legal targets", and in null/wormhole space every ship is a valid target, so on the documented mechanic nothing would exclude friendlies — but I found no source that states the behaviour for friendlies explicitly. Do not assert it as confirmed.
- Design note from the wiki: the very long optimal range is "probably intended to avoid chaining your own entities near your ship and other allies' Vorton Projector users" — i.e. friendly chaining is treated as a live concern. [EVE Uni Wiki — Vorton Projectors](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Vorton_Projectors)

### Other splash / AoE that hits non-locked entities

- **Standup Guided Bombs (Upwell structures).** Unlike normal bombs, these *are* targeted — "the structure gunner needs to target a ship and activate the module on that ship" — with a **20 km area-of-effect radius**, 2,800 omni (light) or 9,600 omni (heavy) damage. "If that ship warps off before it is hit, the bomb just continues to drift in space until its maximum flight time of 400 seconds ends", so it can still catch other ships. [EVE Uni Wiki — Guided Bombs](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Guided_Bombs)
- **Titan AoE superweapons.** Lance (2.5 km radius × 200 km line, up to 50,000 DPS for 15 s, 5 km damage signature), Reaper (5 km radius sweeping beam, up to 100,000 DPS, 3,500 m damage signature), Bosonic Field Generator (30 km cone, up to 40,000 omni DPS, 10 km damage signature). All three additionally release a **10 km radius, 35,000 GJ energy-neutralising pulse** when they begin charging, which affects friend and foe alike. AoE superweapons cannot be activated in lowsec. [EVE Uni Wiki — Titans](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Titans)
- **Doomsday (single-target)** superweapons deal 1,000,000 (1,500,000 at max skills) damage to one capital ship after a 9-second delay — targeted, but with no intervening-object check. [EVE Uni Wiki — Titans](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Titans)

## D. Friendly fire between fleet members

### Fleet membership provides no damage immunity

- **There is no hard game rule preventing damage to a fleetmate.** Fleet membership is an organizational and overview construct; the only gate on dealing damage is *legality*, enforced through safety settings and Crimewatch. Evidence:
  - Drones: "Note that your drones will engage whatever you order them to, **including your other drones or your fleetmates**." The **Assist** command makes drones "assist a member of your fleet, and will engage whatever target they are attacking"; **Guard** makes them "engage whatever ships attacks the fleet member you order your drones to guard". [EVE Uni Wiki — Drones](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Drones)
  - Logistics: "it's important to avoid accidentally shooting corporation members in mixed fleets, **since that can be a legal action in and by itself if the corporation has friendly fire enabled** (which most corporations have)". [EVE Uni Wiki — Remote assistance](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Remote_assistance)
  - In nullsec and wormhole space, "everyone is always a valid target". [CCP — Corp Little Things & Friendly Fire Control](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/corp-little-things-friendly-fire-control)
- Fleet membership therefore *discourages* friendly fire socially and via overview standings icons (fleet members show as a distinct blue icon), but does not mechanically prevent it. [EVE Uni Wiki — EVE University Rules](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Rules_of_engagement) (community corp policy illustrating that the constraint is social, not mechanical)

### Safety settings (green / yellow / red)

- Three settings: **Enable safety** (green) "prevent[s] all actions that would give you suspect or criminal status"; **Partial safety** (yellow) "prevents actions that give criminal status, while allowing those that give suspect status"; **Disable safety** (red) allows actions giving either status. [EVE Uni Wiki — Safety settings](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Safety_settings)
- Constraints: Alpha clones in highsec cannot set safety to red; pilots controlling an Upwell structure in highsec cannot set yellow or red. Changes persist until changed again. (Prior to the 2026-05-22.1 release, safety auto-reset to green on login.) [EVE Uni Wiki — Safety settings](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Safety_settings)
- Regional practice: highsec pilots run green except suicide gankers and smartbomb users who temporarily drop to red; lowsec pilots mostly run yellow (red only for smartbombs, MJFG, or unprovoked capsule attacks); null/wormhole residents can run green permanently because there are no legality issues and "smartbombs as well as bomb launchers both work on full safeties in wormhole and null security space"; logistics pilots are often forced to yellow by the abundance of Limited Engagement timers. [EVE Uni Wiki — Safety settings](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Safety_settings)

### Crimewatch / CONCORD / suspect & criminal flags by security class

- **Highsec.** Activating **any** offensive module on a player's ship, capsule, or assets you are not legitimately allowed to attack — war targets, suspects, criminals, outlaws, duel opponents, limited-engagement opponents being the legitimate cases — **including non-targeted modules like smartbombs**, provokes a **Criminal Timer**: CONCORD spawns and destroys you, sentries fire, anyone may engage you, and your jettisoned wrecks are auto-abandoned. You cannot warp or jump while flagged. [EVE Uni Wiki — Timers / Crimewatch](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Timers)
- **Lowsec.** "Attacking a player's *ship* (not their capsule) with any offensive module, **including Smartbombs and other non-targeted weapons**, will provoke a Suspect Timer." Attacking a *capsule* unprovoked is criminal; a lowsec criminal is not hunted by CONCORD but is fired on by gate/station sentries and may be freely engaged. [EVE Uni Wiki — Timers / Crimewatch](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Timers)
- **Nullsec / wormhole.** "Naturally in null security space or wormhole space, where you are allowed to freely engage anyone at any time, there would be no legality issues and no timers apart from the combat timers would be created in the first place." [EVE Uni Wiki — Timers / Crimewatch](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Timers)
- **Suspect and Criminal flags** both mean "Capsuleers may freely engage" for 15 minutes (Criminal additionally invokes CONCORD in highsec). A **Limited Engagement** (5 minutes) is created when you attack someone under a suspect or criminal flag, or via a duel, and permits both parties to engage each other and each other's assets freely. [EVE Uni Wiki — Timers / Crimewatch](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Timers)
- **Combat (non-legality) timers also apply:** a Capsuleer Log-Off Timer (15 min) from any capsuleer combat, and a Weapons Timer (60 s) from using offensive modules on another capsuleer or activating a bastion/command burst module, which blocks jumping, docking, refitting, and tethering. [EVE Uni Wiki — Timers / Crimewatch](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Timers)
- CCP's own reference section for these mechanics: [CCP Support — Crimewatch](https://support.eveonline.com/hc/en-us/sections/201141682-Crimewatch).

### The CCP news post "Corp Little Things & Friendly Fire Control" — what it actually says

Primary source: [CCP — Corp Little Things & Friendly Fire Control](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/corp-little-things-friendly-fire-control) (CCP Punkturis, 2015-02-09, shipped with the Tiamat release on 2015-02-17).

- **It is a corporation-level legality toggle, not a damage-prevention or immunity system.** CCP gave player corporations "the ability to configure whether 'friendly fire' within the corporation is considered legal, or if it will carry criminal penalties (specifically CONCORD punishment in high-sec, security status hits, Suspect/Criminal flagging etc)". [CCP](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/corp-little-things-friendly-fire-control)
- **What changes when it is set to illegal:** "When set to Illegal, aggressive acts will follow the same rules as they currently do in NPC corporations: attacks will invoke CONCORD (in high sec), trigger a security status penalty and also a Criminal/Suspect flag." The module still fires and the damage still lands — only the legal consequence changes. [CCP](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/corp-little-things-friendly-fire-control)
- **What does not change:** "Duels, kill-rights, outlaw flagging and similar situational events will always provide a way to legally attack corp-mates, and will override the friendly fire setting. The safety system will respect the current crimewatch rules and allow/prevent module activation as expected in any given situation. **In null-sec and wormhole space, everyone is always a valid target, so nothing will change here.**" [CCP](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/corp-little-things-friendly-fire-control)
- **Defaults and administration:** configurable by the CEO or a director in the corporation management window, with a 24-hour activation delay and notifications to all members at the start and completion of the timer; the state is publicly visible in Show Info and filterable in the Corporation Finder; **existing player corporations defaulted to friendly fire being Legal**, so most corps behave as before. [CCP](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/corp-little-things-friendly-fire-control)
- **It is emphatically not about AoE or structure damage.** The post's framing is entirely about *aggression rules between corp members* and the design goal of giving corps "an intermediate choice" between "a corp where other players can shoot you" and "not joining a corp at all" — i.e. a spectrum of risk analogous to hangar access permissions. There is no mention of smartbombs, bombs, structures, or any damage filter. [CCP](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/corp-little-things-friendly-fire-control)
- **Practical answer to "can you shoot your own fleetmate?"** Yes. In nullsec/wormhole always. In lowsec at the cost of a suspect flag. In highsec, only if they are flashy (suspect/criminal/outlaw/war target/duel/limited engagement) or a corp-mate in a corp with friendly fire set to Legal (the default) — otherwise CONCORD destroys you. Fleet membership by itself changes nothing.

### Logistics, remote repair, and "assist" mechanics

- **Remote assistance modules exist:** remote hull/armor repairers, remote shield boosters, remote capacitor transmitters, remote tracking computers, remote sensor boosters — activated on a *friendly* ship. [EVE Uni Wiki — Remote assistance](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Remote_assistance)
- **They inherit timers and legality.** "Whenever you remotely assist someone you will automatically inherit all the combat timers of the one you're assisting" (Non-Capsuleer Log-Off, Capsuleer Log-Off, Weapons Timer). Assisting a suspect/outlaw is usually a Suspect offence; "assisting criminals or interfering in a fight between two parties in high security space (war or limited engagement) is a Criminal offence"; in lowsec, interference is merely Suspect. All of this respects the assisting pilot's safety settings. [EVE Uni Wiki — Remote assistance](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Remote_assistance), [EVE Uni Wiki — Timers / Crimewatch](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Timers)
- **A documented hard exception:** "While a warp disruption field generator is active, the ship activating it suffers a 50% increase to its signature radius, and **cannot be targeted by allied remote repairs or capacitor transmitters**." This is one of the very few rules in EVE that mechanically blocks a friendly action — and it is a module-state restriction, not a fleet-membership rule. [EVE Uni Wiki — Tackling](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Tackling)
- **Command Bursts are legality-gated in highsec:** "Even Command Bursts are affected by legality in high security space. Only members of the same corporation or alliance (or on the same side in faction warfare) will receive boosts if they have a Capsuleer Log-Off Timer while being at war or in a limited engagement." In low/null/wormhole, boosts are unaffected by legality. [EVE Uni Wiki — Remote assistance](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Remote_assistance)
- **Assist mechanics do exist fleet-side.** Drone **Assist** assigns your drones to a fleet member and they engage whatever that member attacks; **Guard** engages whatever attacks them. Drones are commanded through the drone control window and require the target to be within drone control range (20 km base, up to 60 km with Drone Avionics V and Advanced Drone Avionics V, further with Drone Link Augmentors and rigs). [EVE Uni Wiki — Drones](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Drones)
- **Cap chains** are the logistics counterpart: Guardians and Basilisks must lock each other and establish a mutual capacitor chain, which is why they must start moving immediately after exiting warp so their fleetmates can lock them past the Warp Invulnerability Timer. [EVE Uni Wiki — Timers](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Timers)

## E. Cover: **EVE has none**

- **No cover mechanic exists.** There is no line-of-sight test anywhere in EVE's targeting or damage pipeline. Hit chance depends on range, tracking, and signature radius; lock acquisition depends on range, sensor strength, and scan resolution. Asteroids, stations, structures, and other ships are not consulted. A ship parked directly behind a Keepstar is exactly as easy to lock and hit as one in open space. [EVE Uni Wiki — Turret mechanics](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Turret_mechanics), [EVE Uni Wiki — Missile mechanics](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Missile_mechanics)
- **What objects *do* block is movement, not fire.** Ships collide and bump off structures, asteroids, and each other; this affects positioning and can be weaponized (bumping a target off a docking ring, for example) but has no effect on whether shots connect. Collision is a navigation/geometry system, not a cover system. (Discussed by players in [Objects obstructing line of sight?](https://forums.eveonline.com/t/objects-obstrcuting-line-of-sight/350354) — label: community, and note the thread is a feature *request* premised on cover not existing.)
- **Player requests for cover/LoS have not been implemented.** The 2022 feature-request thread is the clearest public evidence: the premise of the thread is "Why does Eve's physics not account for objects/collide-ables like stations, asteroids when it comes to weapons hitting targets… If I am in an asteroid field could I not use cover to avoid gankers?" Replies (community) explain that EVE's damage calculations are server-side sphere/range math and that adding occlusion would require per-shot intersection tests. [Objects obstructing line of sight?](https://forums.eveonline.com/t/objects-obstrcuting-line-of-sight/350354) — label: community.
- **Do not confuse cover with tackling.** Warp denial is a completely separate axis from fire occlusion:
  - **Interdiction Sphere Launcher / Warp Disrupt Probe:** creates a warp disruption bubble with a **20 km radius** lasting two minutes; "All ships within this field cannot activate their warp drives to leave, however they can otherwise move freely." Ships inside a bubble can still shoot and be shot normally. The probe can itself be destroyed by smartbombs. Cannot be activated in high or low security space. [EVE Uni Wiki — Tackling](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Tackling)
  - **Heavy Interdictor warp disruption field generator:** unscripted mode creates a bubble centered on the generating ship (null/wormhole only); scripted modes give a 100-point "infinity point" disruptor or scrambler usable anywhere. [EVE Uni Wiki — Tackling](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Tackling)
  - **Warp disruptors / scramblers / stasis webifiers** pin a ship in place or slow it; none of them block, absorb, or intercept fire. [EVE Uni Wiki — Tackling](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Tackling)
  - **"Gate camp"** is therefore a *tackle* tactic — campers hold a chokepoint (a stargate) with points/scrams/webs (and often bubbles in nullsec) so passing ships cannot warp away, then apply damage. It is not cover, and the gate itself provides no protection. [EVE Uni Wiki — Tackling](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Tackling)
- **The nearest legitimate "terrain" analogues** are therefore all non-cover: warp-in geometry (celestials, bookmarks, perch spots), grid boundaries, range control, and LoS-free positioning to minimize angular velocity ("spiral in", "keep at range", matching velocity vectors) — all of which manipulate the *hit-chance equation's* inputs rather than blocking shots. [EVE Uni Wiki — Turret mechanics](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Turret_mechanics)
- **Titan Lance is the one weapon whose AoE is drawn as a line**, and it is worth flagging in a design report precisely because it looks like line-of-fire geometry while being nothing of the sort: it is a fixed 200 km × 2.5 km radius damage template that hits everything inside it, with no occlusion and no friendly filter. [EVE Uni Wiki — Titans](https://wiki.eveuniversity.org/Titans)
